Follow the chain of trust

A browser trusts a site's key because a chain of signatures leads from the site's certificate to a root certificate it already holds. Build a small chain, let the browser check each link, then break a link and watch the browser refuse.

    The certificates here are simplified to their subject, issuer, public key and signature, and the names are roles, not real authorities. The signatures are real: your browser's own cryptography, called Web Crypto, makes the keys and checks every link, using elliptic-curve signatures because they are quick to make.
