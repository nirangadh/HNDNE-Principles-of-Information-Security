A certificate is a passport

This page opens a real certificate, the one provided for this afternoon's lab, and reads it field by field. Beside each field is the matching part of a passport. A certificate in the X.509 format says that a public key belongs to a name, and someone signs to vouch for it.

Step 3

Check the stamp

The last field is the issuer's signature over every other field. Here the issuer is the subject itself, so the certificate is checked with its own public key.

Step 4

The fingerprint

The fingerprint is the SHA-256 digest of the whole certificate. Your browser shows the same kind of value in its certificate viewer, so you can check by eye that two copies are the same certificate.

This page reads the certificate's bytes itself and uses your browser's own cryptography, called Web Crypto, to check the signature and take the fingerprint. The certificate is self-signed: it is valid in form, but no authority in your browser's trust store vouches for it. That is why a browser warns about it on a website.
