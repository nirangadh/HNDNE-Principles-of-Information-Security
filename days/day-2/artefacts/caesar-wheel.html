The Caesar wheel

The Caesar shift moves every letter the same number of places along the alphabet. The method is public. The key is the number of places. Turn the wheel to encrypt a message, then try every key and see how quickly the message comes back.

Now forget the key. Take the text after the wheel and try all twenty-five keys, one after another. This is called brute force.

    A shift of 26 brings every letter back to itself, so there are only twenty-five real keys. A shift of 13 has its own name, ROT13 (rotate, or shift, by thirteen): applying it twice gives back the message. The messages are prepared examples from Taxila College, which is fictional, and nothing on this page is sent anywhere.
