A pinch of salt

Systems store a digest of each password, never the password. But common passwords have been hashed in advance and kept in lookup tables. This page holds a small lookup table of about a hundred common passwords. See what it finds, and what a salt does to it.

Step 4

Slow on purpose

A fast hash lets a guesser try many passwords a second. A slow password hash, such as the Password-Based Key Derivation Function 2 (PBKDF2), repeats its work many times. Time one of each, using the lecturer's salt from step 2.

This page uses your browser's own cryptography, called Web Crypto, for every digest and for PBKDF2. The table matches digests computed in advance; it never reverses one. What you paste stays on this page and is only compared with the table. A real system chooses each salt at random, as step 2 does. In the lab, your salts come from your index number, so your lecturer can check them.
