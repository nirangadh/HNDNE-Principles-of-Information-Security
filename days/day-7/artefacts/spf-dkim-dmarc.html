Three checks that fight spoofing

A receiving server cannot trust a from line, so it checks what the domain has published. Turn Taxila's three records on one at a time, then deliver three prepared messages and read what each check says. The Sender Policy Framework (SPF) lists the servers that may send; DomainKeys Identified Mail (DKIM) is a signature; Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting and Conformance (DMARC) is the rule for failures.

This page uses your browser's Web Crypto to make two real RSA key pairs when it opens: one for taxila.example and one for the look-alike sis-taxila.example. The genuine message and the look-alike message are really signed (RSASSA-PKCS1-v1_5 with SHA-256, as DKIM's rsa-sha256), and the DKIM check really verifies each signature with the published public key. The keys never leave your browser. Every address is a prepared sample, and nothing is sent anywhere.
