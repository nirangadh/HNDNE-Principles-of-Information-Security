# Principles of Information Security

**Higher National Diploma in Network Engineering, Year 2**

Over ten days you will learn how information is protected, one idea at a time, and each idea is shown working before it is named. You start with what is worth protecting and why. You then learn how keys keep secrets, how hashes prove that nothing changed, and how certificates let strangers trust each other. Then you see how systems check who you are and what you may do, and how web applications, email, databases and networks are attacked and defended. The last teaching day covers what an organisation does when protection fails. Every day adds to the security programme of **Taxila College**, a fictional private college in Sri Lanka, where you join the IT office as a trainee security analyst.

**Before Day 1, read the getting-started guide** (posted with the prelude). It shows you how to set up your laptop and explains the rules for the tools you will use.

## What you will be able to do

1. Explain the fundamentals of information security.
2. Discuss the key characteristics of cryptography and cryptographic systems.
3. Analyze the principles of encryption, decryption, hashing, and data integrity.
4. Outline the process of ensuring digital security.
5. Describe the fundamentals of threats and countermeasures.
6. Explain the fundamentals and process of business continuity planning.

## How a teaching day works

In the morning you build the ideas, step by step, with a short demo for each step. In the afternoon you use the ideas in a lab or an activity on your own laptop. Every lab has a path for any laptop with a browser, and a path for laptops that can run a Kali Linux virtual machine. Every lab sheet has the same shape: numbered steps that tell you what you should see, a **prove it** step, a **break it** step where you make a fault on purpose and find it, a **reset** list, and the **rules of engagement** for any tool that could attack a system.
