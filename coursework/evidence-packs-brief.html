Individual evidence packs

This brief explains the five evidence packs you submit after Days 2 to 6: what goes into each pack, how each pack is marked, and the rules that keep each pack your own work.

Weight: 25% of the module. Individual.

What you submit

Five evidence packs, one after each of Days 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6. Each pack is due before the next teaching day; dates are announced for your cohort. Submit each pack through the learning management system (LMS) as one PDF. The five packs count equally. A pack that is not submitted scores zero.

Every pack has the same three parts. The lab sheet for the day tells you exactly what each part contains.

  1. Your personal values. Each lab uses your index number, written without spaces or hyphens, as its input: the message you encrypt, the salt you add, the file you sign. Your results are therefore different from every other student's, and your lecturer can recompute them. Results made from someone else's index number, or from the example in the lab sheet, score zero for this part.
  2. A concept sketch, drawn by hand. Draw the day's mechanism on paper, label the parts the lab sheet names, and photograph it. Neat drawing is not marked; correct labels are. A sketch drawn on a computer, or copied from a slide, scores zero for this part.
  3. A short explanation. Answer the lab sheet's one "why" question in your own words, in no more than 120 words. Simple English is fine. The ideas are marked, not the language.

You can earn every mark on any laptop. The labs have a path that needs only a browser, and the full-tier path with a Kali Linux virtual machine earns no extra marks.

How each pack is marked

Each pack is marked out of 20.

Part Marks Full marks look like
Completeness 4 Every item the lab sheet lists is present and labelled with its step
Personal values 8 Every value is correct for your index number, and you show how you got it
Concept sketch 5 Hand-drawn; every part the lab sheet names is present and correctly labelled; the arrows show the right direction
Explanation 3 Answers the "why", names the mechanism, and stays within 120 words

Rules

  • Your pack must be your own work. The academic integrity rules of your programme apply, including its rules on generated text.
  • Work with others in the lab if you like, but compute your own values, draw your own sketch, and write your own explanation.
  • Late packs follow the standard late-submission rules.
