Group tabletop exercise: an incident at Taxila College

This brief explains the group exercise: how your group prepares across Days 6 to 9, what happens in the live round on Day 10 morning, what you submit afterwards, and how it is marked.

Weight: 25% of the module. Group size: four (three or five where the cohort needs it). Groups form: on Day 5. Preparation: across Days 6 to 9. Live round: Day 10 morning. Submission: after Day 10. Exact dates are announced for your cohort.

1. The task

A tabletop exercise is how real organisations practise for a bad day: the team sits round a table, someone describes an incident as it unfolds, and the team decides what to do, step by step, before it happens for real.

Your group is the security team at Taxila College. Before Day 10 you prepare: you list Taxila's risks and the controls that answer them. On Day 10 your lecturer runs a live incident for your group. New events arrive every few minutes. You decide what to do, write each action and its reason in your action log, and keep the college running.

2. Roles

Each member holds one role for the live round. Agree the roles on Day 5.

Role Leads on
Incident lead keeps the action log, sets priorities, calls the next step
Technical lead what is happening in the systems, and which control stops it
Communications lead who must be told what (the Registrar, staff, students, the Data Protection Officer), and when
Continuity lead which services must come back first, how, and how much data could be lost

In a group of three, the incident lead also leads communications. In a group of five, two members share the technical role.

3. Preparation (Days 6 to 9)

Build these two items as you go; each day's afternoon gives you material for them.

  1. A risk register for Taxila of at least ten risks. For each risk: the asset, the threat, the vulnerability, likelihood and impact, the property most at stake (confidentiality, integrity, availability, authenticity or accountability), and the treatment you choose.
  2. A controls map: Taxila's security register, with each control you rely on, the risks it answers, and whether it prevents, detects or corrects.

Bring both to the live round, printed or on one laptop. They are submitted at the start of the round.

4. The live round (Day 10 morning)

About 45 minutes per group. Your lecturer reads out the incident and then adds events one at a time. For each event, your group agrees an action, and the incident lead writes it in the action log with its reason and the time. You may use your risk register, your controls map and your notes. At the end, each member answers one short question about a role that was not their own.

5. After the round

Within the period announced for your cohort, the group submits:

  1. An after-action report of no more than 1,500 words: what happened, what you did and why, what went well, what you would change, and which items in Taxila's security register helped or failed.
  2. An updated continuity plan for the services the incident touched: their priority, recovery time objective (RTO) and recovery point objective (RPO), and the steps to bring each back.
  3. A contribution statement signed by all members.

6. Marking

Criterion Weight Full marks look like
Preparation 30% Risks are specific to Taxila; every risk names its property and a sensible treatment; every control is placed and classified correctly
Live round 30% Actions are timely, follow priorities, and are justified in the log; the group uses its own register and map
After-action report and continuity plan 25% Honest about what failed; RTO and RPO argued from the scenario; recovery steps in a workable order
Individual question 15% Each member answers correctly about a role other than their own

A peer-assessment factor of plus or minus 10% may be applied to an individual's share where the contribution statement and the live round justify it and programme regulations permit.
